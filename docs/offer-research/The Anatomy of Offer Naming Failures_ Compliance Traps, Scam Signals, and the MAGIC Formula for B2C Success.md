### The Anatomy of Offer Naming Failures: Compliance Traps, Scam Signals, and the MAGIC Formula for B2C Success

#### 1\. Executive Summary: The Psychological Weight of a Name

In the high-stakes landscape of B2C marketing, an offer name is not a mere label; it is a critical  **strategic asset**  and the primary cognitive container for value. It serves as the initial point of contact with a prospect's inherent  **scepticism** , functioning either as a bridge of trust or a terminal trigger for "scam signals" cite: 1, 2\. When a name lacks specificity or relies on unsubstantiated hyperbole, it erodes perceived authority and signals to the consumer that the underlying service is a commodity or, worse, a fraud cite: 10, 11.Strategic clarity is a prerequisite for survival. Vague naming conventions create insurmountable cognitive friction, while aggressive, non-compliant names invite  **institutional liability** . In the current regulatory environment, the penalty for deceptive naming includes the freezing of merchant accounts and permanent bans across major advertising platforms cite: 7\. This document provides the architectural blueprint for transitioning from the "Black Box" of generic naming to a structured,  **MAGIC-compliant**  framework that marries psychological persuasion with total regulatory adherence.

#### 2\. The 'Black Box' Trap: Why Generic and Vague Names Kill Conversions

A catastrophic strategic failure in the wellness and coaching niches is the failure to title the plan. For intangible services, titling the transformation is a fundamental requirement to make the offer feel  **tangible**  to the consumer cite: 8\. The "Black Box" failure mode occurs when practitioners name offers after their deliverables—such as "60-Minute Coaching"—rather than the objective cite: 12.This approach generates significant  **psychological friction** , forcing the prospect to perform the cognitive heavy lifting of imagining the result themselves cite: 10\. To eliminate this burden, the practitioner must adopt the role of the "Guide" who provides a clear, named path to the "Hero's" desired outcome cite: 10\. Buyers only feel "safe" in their purchase decision when the name defines exactly what they are acquiring and the specific goal they will walk away having completed cite: 8.Furthermore, "Inside Joke" naming or self-centred branding (e.g., "The Zenith Method") lacks the resonance of  **customer-centric, outcome-oriented naming**  cite: 12\. Effective naming must focus on the "Goal Outcome"—the clear, healthy, or profitable state the client reaches cite: 8\. When practitioners fail to achieve this clarity, they frequently pivot to the "Scam Signal" territory of exaggerated claims.

#### 3\. The 'Scam' Signals: Exaggerated Outcomes and Fabricated Value Claims

The regulatory jurisdiction, led by the FTC, maintains a strict mandate against deceptive practices. Cold traffic is highly attuned to the  **"absolute outcome" trap** , where phrases like "Guaranteed Profits" or "Instant Healing" trigger algorithmic semantic matching and invite investigations under Section 5 of the FTC Act cite: 3, 7\.

##### The Value Equation and Regulatory Risk

The synthesis of Alex Hormozi’s  **Value Equation**  (Value \= Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood / Time Delay × Effort) provides a lens for regulatory risk cite: 11\. When a name inflates the "Dream Outcome" but provides no evidence for the "Perceived Likelihood of Achievement," it creates a  **"False Bargain"**  scenario. The FTC views these unsubstantiated anchors as deceptive pricing mechanisms because the consumer is led to believe they are receiving an unusual value that does not exist in reality cite: 3, 5\.

##### Deceptive Pricing and 16 CFR Part 233

Compliance with  **16 CFR Part 233**  is non-negotiable for practitioners using price comparisons or reductions:

1. **Bona Fide Pricing:**  Any "former price" must be the actual price at which the service was openly and actively offered to the public for a reasonably substantial period—typically a 30-day window cite: 3, 5\.  
2. **Fictitious Inflation:**  Establishing an artificial, inflated price for a few days to enable a subsequent "large reduction" is a violation of Guide I cite: 5\.  
3. **Meaningless Reductions:**  Reductions must be sufficiently large that a consumer would believe a genuine saving is offered. Labelling a £100 service as "Reduced to £99.99" is considered misleading cite: 5\.  
4. **Comparable Value:**  Comparisons to "Price Elsewhere" must be based on fact and represent the prevailing price for similar grade and quality services in the trade area cite: 6\.

#### 4\. Copywriting and Ad Compliance Minefields on Meta

Meta operates as a primary enforcement jurisdiction for health and finance advertisers. Its  **Personal Attributes Policy**  prohibits ads from asserting or implying knowledge of a user's medical conditions or struggles cite: 7\. The  **"Second-Person 'You' Trap"** —addressing struggles directly (e.g., "Are you in debt?")—is flagged as a privacy violation by AI crawlers cite: 7\.

#### 5\. Constructing the Correct Cognitive Container: The MAGIC Naming Formula

To balance psychological appeal with regulatory safety, practitioners should deploy a structured naming system. The  **MAGIC Formula**  (Magnet, Avatar, Goal, Interval, Container Word) provides the framework for creating irresistible, compliant offers cite: 10\.

##### The Role of the "Container Word"

Choosing a  **Container Word**  (e.g., Blueprint, System, Ritual) serves as a sophisticated tool for  **Price Anchoring**  cite: 9, 10\. It signifies the offer's unique format and suggests the service is a transformative asset rather than a commodity, which justifies premium pricing cite: 11\. This frames the "Effort" as part of a structured process, increasing the "Perceived Likelihood" of the outcome cite: 11\.

#### 6\. Empirical Validation: Testing and Auditing Offer Names Prior to Launch

High-value marketing requires the transition from arbitrary creative choices to  **data-driven validation**  cite: 1\. Before a name is deployed at scale, it must be audited through empirical testing:

1. **Simplicity Audit:**  The name must communicate the "Goal Outcome" in the fewest words possible to ensure the prospect can visualise the result cite: 8\.  
2. **A/B Split Testing:**  Use version-controlled testing to try two different name variations (e.g., different Container Words or Goals) to see which performs better cite: 1\.  
3. **Performance Metrics:**  Success must be measured through actual  **Click-Through Rates (CTR)** ,  **Cost Per Lead (CPL)** , and  **Return On Ad Spend (ROAS)**  rather than intuition cite: 1, 2.Achieving market resonance is the direct result of the marriage between strategic clarity and total regulatory compliance. Through disciplined naming, a practitioner transforms their marketing from an institutional liability into a predictable, revenue-generating machine.
