## What the node produces

The free asset a cold Meta stranger trades an email for. Its job is narrow and it is NOT to sell: solve
one acute symptom fast, then make one more free step the obvious move. High-ticket is sold later,
off-page. Any output that funnels to a discovery call or a paid offer is wrong for ZAP.

## The core structure, loop-splicing

Every lead magnet does two things at once. Close the minor loop: deliver the specific acute symptom
relief the opt-in promised, within minutes. Open the major loop: at the moment of resolution, name the
systemic root cause the reader had not considered, as a diagnostic question rather than a pitch. Then
offer the next FREE step that resolves the major loop — free training, webinar, report or challenge.
Why it works is the Zeigarnik Effect: the brain holds unfinished loops in active memory and purges
finished ones. A complete self-contained solution triggers psychological discharge and the brand is
forgotten. That is the dead-end failure and it is the single most common reason a good free asset
produces nothing.

## Two dead-end traps to guard against in code

Over-generosity: the whole framework dumped into forty pages, which closes every loop and reads as
homework. The pitch trap: a thin two-page checklist bolted to a paid pitch, where the jump from free to
paid triggers defensive walls.

## The cascade, Node 4 feeds Node 5

The research's teach-the-what-not-the-how principle maps onto the mechanism Node 4 already extracts.
The what, free, is the bird's-eye view of the coach's Unique Mechanism of the Solution — the
milestones, the obstacles, why the old way fails. The how, paid and later, is customised execution,
feedback and accountability. So the major loop the lead magnet opens should be the Unique Mechanism of
the Problem that Node 4 already names. The magnet closes a symptom loop, reveals the UMP, and points to
the free event where the UMS is taught. Same mechanism, same thread: ad, landing page, lead magnet,
free event.

## The 80/20 bar, resolved

ZAP's shipped prompt sets roughly 80 percent immediately-usable tool and 20 percent teaching. The
research puts step-by-step execution on the paid side. These are not opposed. What the research
withholds is the tailored-to-you part — feedback, adjustment, accountability — not any actionable step
whatsoever. The same reports demand actionable gratification, ready-to-send scripts and templates, and
state the success ratio as 20 percent framework and 80 percent actionable tool in as many words. So
80/20 governs delivery format and teach-the-what governs scope. Decision taken: keep 80/20, redefine
the 20 percent. It is not throat-clearing or a mini-lecture. It is the root-cause frame that names the
UMP and hands off to the free next step.

## Format selection, three filters in order

Stage of the journey: unaware of the root cause gives a diagnostic quiz or scorecard; actively seeking
relief gives a single-problem cheat sheet or bracketed template; comparing and evaluating fit gives a
mini-video training or email course. Practitioner strength: writes well and hates camera gives an
email course or cheat sheet; strong conversational presence gives mini-video or interactive scorecard;
never assign a format the coach cannot actually produce. Mobile sizing: consumable and actionable in
one short sitting on a phone.

## Length and craft

One audience, one problem, one quick win — breadth dilutes. Useful beats comprehensive: length
dictated by the problem, not by page count. Format to length: checklist seven to fifteen action items;
template one to three pages; guide three to twenty pages. Specificity over generic titles — "The
24-Hour Cash Flow Audit" beats "The Complete Guide to Business". Teaching sequence when teaching is
needed: problem, solution, three to five actionable steps, next step. The dead-end test: does the
asset make the next step the logical move, or does it close the loop and end the relationship.

## The free next step, decided 2026-08-25

The cascade generates the free next step itself rather than capturing a URL from the coach, because
one of twenty-three coaches has even a booking URL and asking for a destination they do not have
returns an honest dead end with extra steps. A lead-magnet campaign also produces a
webinar_registration landing page on the same service, and the magnet's nextStepUrl resolves to its
publicUrl. Webinar was chosen because a page type that structurally cannot display a price beats one
that can and must be told not to, and because its three questions — date, time, timezone — are all
answerable before a coach has built anything.

## The source tier chain for destinations, the Node 4 pattern applied

One: sibling campaign, a free-type campaign in the same service, model-native and best when present.
Two: operator-captured URL, the coach's own destination asked for at publish. Three: no destination,
render the card as text with no button, never invent, an honest dead end.

## A finding worth carrying into any prompt change

A prohibition loses to a later instruction that demands words. One framing said to give the date and
time of the session and nothing else. A later item demanded 200 to 300 words in a field called
Scarcity/Urgency. A date is ten words, so the generator had to find 240 more and had nothing true to
spend them on, and invented seat caps, cohort schedules and waitlists in five of five rows under a
framing that explicitly forbade them. That is not two instructions competing. It is a contradiction
with a word count attached, and the count wins because it is the only one that can be satisfied.
