# ZAP — How the System Generates Meta Ad Copy Today

**A factual, as-built description of the live production system**, taken from the source code (branch `railway-build`, commit `51eda78`), not from documentation. This document exists to be compared, source-against-source, with research on how Meta's "Andromeda" retrieval engine reads and categorises ad copy. It describes only what the system *does* — it makes no claims about what it *should* do.

---

## 0. Overview and vocabulary

ZAP generates ad copy in **two separate places** that produce different things and never share a prompt:

- **Node 6 — Headlines:** a standalone generator producing long, formula-driven headlines (with optional eyebrow and subheadline lines). These are the headlines a coach browses and picks from.
- **Node 7 — Ad Copy:** produces three different things in one run — short headlines, body copy (Meta's "primary text"), and link descriptions.

There is **no separate "hook" generator**. In this system a hook is not a distinct field or record — it is the *opening line of the body copy*, shaped by the angle and awareness-stage guidance given to that body copy. Where this document says "hook," it means the opening of the body text.

(A separate "concept engine" does produce named hook patterns, but it feeds video scripts and concept records — **not** the two ad-copy nodes. See Section 5.)

---

## 1. Node 6 — Headlines

### 1.1 What it produces
Five headline **formulas**, each generated by its own independent language-model call, all five running simultaneously. Each formula produces 5 headlines by default → **25 headlines per standard run**. "Lite mode" ×0.4 (2 per formula, 10 total); "power mode" ×3 (15 per formula, 75 total). A coach may also request a single formula.

Each stored headline has three text fields — a main headline, an optional subheadline, and an optional eyebrow (a short line above the headline) — plus the formula it came from, the four inputs used, a compliance score, a selection score, and any policy violations found. The model is forced to return structured data matching a fixed schema, so it cannot return prose or malformed output.

The five formulas, with their exact instructions:

- **Story** — *"How a [Triggering Event] Led/Pushed/Triggered a [Person] to [Discovery] that [Result]!"* Use varied triggering events; make the person relatable to the target market; each headline 15–25 words.
- **Eyebrow** — three parts. Eyebrow: *"[Authority] Unveils/Reveals."* Main: *"[Unique Mechanism] Turns [Audience] into [Result]."* Subheadline: *"Without [Pain 1], [Pain 2] or [Pain 3]."* Eyebrow establishes authority; main features the unique mechanism; subheadline addresses three pain points.
- **Question** — *"[Question about obstacle/mistake]?"* Frame as a question about the obstacle itself — why it persists, what it quietly costs. Use words like "preventing," "stopping," "sabotaging," "sapping." 10–20 words.
- **Authority** — Main: *"[Authority Figure] [Action] [Unique Mechanism] [Result]."* Subheadline: *"This is why [Old Way 1], [Old Way 2], and [Old Way 3] have failed to produce [Desired Outcome]."* Action verbs: unearthed, discovered, revealed, disclosed, unveiled. A hard override forbids inventing credentials — the model must use real supplied credentials, bracketed placeholders (e.g. `[INSERT_AUTHORITY_TITLE]`), or generic role framing.
- **Urgency** — *"[Action] [Unique Mechanism], and [Result] in [Timeframe]!"* Opens with Discover/Unearth/Leverage/Unlock/Access. Requires a specific timeframe and concrete outcome language (scale, build, deliver, claim, secure). Explicitly forbids clickbait verbs (skyrocket, explode, rains, dominate, crush).

### 1.2 Inputs
Four core inputs are substituted into every formula: **target market, pressing problem, desired outcome, unique mechanism** (the last three fall back to the coach's service record if left blank). Four further blocks are prepended:

- **Brand context** (the coach's "source of truth" record): core offer, target audience, main pain, main benefits, unique value, avatar. Framed as "the approved brand voice; all copy must be consistent with this."
- **Ideal Customer Profile** — three fields only: daily pains, deep fears, and what makes them buy.
- **Campaign-type context** — one of seven fixed paragraphs (webinar, discovery call, challenge, course launch, product launch, lead magnet, in-person event). E.g. webinar: "give a reason to attend live — sell the event itself… avoid evergreen-funnel language." Discovery call: "invite a 1:1 conversation… selectivity framing — application, qualification, fit-check."
- **Cascade context** — the upstream assets already chosen: the selected offer, unique method, and lead magnet.

### 1.3 Generation logic
All five formulas run **simultaneously and independently**. No formula sees the others' output, so there is **no cross-checking for repetition across the set**. Each is a single call with no redraft loop.

**There is no awareness-stage handling anywhere in Node 6.** No stage is selected, passed, or referenced. No hook pattern is assigned. Every one of the 25 headlines is generated from the same context with the same instructions, varying only by formula.

### 1.4 Rules, filters and constraints
- **The three-question test** (applied to every headline): (1) Does it name a specific person in a specific situation? (2) Does it promise a specific outcome, not a vague benefit? (3) Could this headline *only* be written for THIS service?
- **Twelve banned openers:** Discover · Unlock · Transform · Imagine · Are you ready · It's time to · Say goodbye to · Tired of · Are you struggling with · Finally · The secret to · How to finally — plus "Everything you need to…" and generic power words (skyrocket, explode, dominate, crush it, master).
- **Mandatory ICP-vocabulary rule:** every headline must contain at least one word drawn from the ICP's pain/desire/niche vocabulary — a word that signals "this was written for me specifically."
- **A "register standard"** attached to every call: copy speaks from what the coach has lived, seen and built; recognition comes from accuracy of the described moment, not from telling the reader what is true of them.
- **Scoring.** Every headline receives a **compliance score** (starts at 100; −20 per critical violation, −8 per warning; text inside quotation marks is skipped; "compliant" = no critical violation and ≥70; bands 90+/70+/50+) and a **selection score** (starts at 50; +10 each for containing a digit, not opening with a banned word, being 5–12 words, containing "you," etc.).
- **Nothing is deleted at this node.** Low-scoring headlines are stored with their violation reasons. Optionally (behind a switch that is **off by default**), headlines scoring under 70 get a compliant rewrite stored alongside. The first headline is auto-selected into the campaign kit.

## 2. Node 7 — Ad Copy (short headlines + body copy + link descriptions)

### 2.1 What it produces
Three distinct output types in one run, stored as separate rows tagged by type:

| Type | Count (default / lite / power) | Length limit |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | 15 / 3 / 30 | max 40 characters |
| Body copy | one per angle slot, up to 18 / 3 in lite | 125–150 words |
| Link description | 15 / 3 / 30 | max 30 characters |

A standard run produces roughly **46 pieces of copy**. Each row stores the copy, its type, the body angle (if applicable), every input field used, a compliance score, a selection score, and violation reasons.

### 2.2 Inputs
Everything Node 6 receives, plus: service name and category, product category, specific product name, key benefits, specific technology, scientific studies, credible authority, ad type, ad style, and the chosen call-to-action. Missing fields become bracketed placeholders (e.g. `[INSERT_UNIQUE_MECHANISM]`) rather than being invented. Two further inputs matter:

- **Proof data** — testimonials, review counts, customer totals, "featured in" credits. Whether real proof exists changes which angles are allowed (see 2.4).
- **Cascade context** — for this node includes the selected offer, unique method, lead magnet, and **the selected headline from Node 6**, passed as background context (not a constraint).

### 2.3 Order of production, and what knows what
The three types are produced in sequence by three separate calls: **(1)** headlines first, awaited; **(2)** body copies next, all angles in parallel; **(3)** link descriptions last. **None of the three sees the others' output.** The body copy does not receive the headlines that were just generated; the link descriptions receive neither. They are coherent only because they share the same upstream context. **There is no rule anywhere that a headline must differ from, complement, or avoid repeating the body's opening line.**

### 2.4 The 18 body angles
Every body copy is written to one named angle: pain agitation · social proof · authority · curiosity · story · urgency · benefit stack · comparison · question · guarantee · transformation · contrarian · data-driven · emotional · direct-response — plus three "PDC" angles: pain, desire, circumstance.

Every angle is written in **first person by default** (the coach's own experience). This was a deliberate change from earlier versions that addressed the reader diagnostically ("You know that feeling when…") — the exact mechanism Meta's personal-attributes policy prohibits. **Proof-dependent angles are withheld** from coaches with no supplied proof (they are structurally built around a client account, so a coach without one would have to invent it).

### 2.5 Awareness-stage handling (the only place stages operate)
Each **body-copy slot** is assigned a Schwartz awareness stage from a fixed cold-traffic weighting:

| Stage | Weight |
|---|---|
| Unaware | 3 |
| Problem-Aware | 3 |
| Solution-Aware | 1 |
| Product-Aware | 1 |
| Most-Aware | 0 |

Slots are distributed by proportional apportionment; leftover slots go to the stages with the largest fractional remainder, and only to stages with non-zero weight (so Most-Aware never picks one up by rounding).

Each stage maps to a preferred body angle, with fallbacks: Unaware → curiosity (fallback story, data-driven) · Problem-Aware → pain agitation (contrarian, story) · Solution-Aware → transformation (comparison, authority) · Product-Aware → social proof (data-driven, guarantee) · Most-Aware → urgency (direct-response). On a full 18-slot deck the mapped angles run out (four stages × three angles cannot cover 18), so a slot takes the next unused angle but keeps its assigned stage guidance.

Each slot receives a paragraph of stage guidance. The **Unaware** paragraph in full: *"This reader does not yet have words for the problem. They are scrolling for entertainment, not solutions. Open a knowledge gap and let curiosity carry them — an educational entry point, a short story or an observation that lands as native to the feed. Keep the offer in the background; the job of this copy is recognition, not the sale. Lead with the counterintuitive observation, so the first lines carry the specific subject matter rather than a generic opening (the platform reads the opening tokens to classify what this ad is about)."* **Solution-Aware:** "Lead with the mechanism of change — what this method does differently and why that difference produces the result." **Most-Aware:** "Urgency comes only from a genuine deadline present in the supplied material; where none exists, close on the concrete next step instead."

**The headlines and link descriptions in this node receive no stage assignment. Only body copy does.**

### 2.6 Body-copy structure
A fixed four-part shape: **PAIN** (1–2 sentences, the specific moment the coach knows this problem by) → **AGITATE** (2–3 sentences, what it costs, which usual fixes fail) → **SOLUTION** (2–3 sentences, introduce the mechanism, name what makes it different, a specific outcome/timeframe from supplied material) → **CTA** (1 sentence, one clear next step, approved CTA format). Formatting: max 15 words/sentence (absolute 20); max 2 sentences/paragraph; blank line between paragraphs; Grade-6 reading level.

### 2.7 Node 7's own headline rules (different from Node 6)
- **Scroll-stopper rule:** the first word must arrest the scroll — open on the specific situation or a counterintuitive insight, not a benefit or the service name.
- A differently worded three-question test (specific situation vs category; specific outcome vs category of outcome; only-for-this-service), a mandatory pressing-problem word, and the twelve banned openers with a slightly longer power-word list (skyrocket, explode, dominate, crush, master, unlock, transform).
- The set is spread across five or six named angles: pain, situation, curiosity, mechanism, contrast, and (only where real proof exists) social proof.
- Requires a results qualifier on any outcome claim, honest benefit language, and one approved CTA ("Learn More," "Sign Up," "Book a Call," "Get Started," "Download Free Guide," "Watch Free Training"). Supplies six worked reframes, e.g. "Make $10k/month" → "Build a sustainable coaching income"; "Secret method" → "A counterintuitive approach that most coaches overlook." Closes with: *"Your output must be ad copy that could be submitted directly to Meta without triggering a policy violation review."*

### 2.8 The compliance block
A long rules block on every call forbids ten categories: income/earnings claims; guaranteed results; specific transformation claims; unqualified superlatives; sensationalist language ("shocking secret," "what doctors won't tell you"); false urgency ("only 3 spots left" unless literally true); before/after language; **personal-attribute targeting** (singling out age, religion, race, sexual orientation, disability, health, or financial hardship); misleading accuracy; and **attributes of a person** — health, body, mental state, finances, age, background, circumstances — may be described about the coach's own experience but never stated or implied about the reader. A no-credential-fabrication rule and general Meta-compliance notes are also attached.

### 2.9 The banned-word list (42 terms, all generated copy in this node)
transformation, journey, potential, unlock, empower, breakthrough, passion, purpose, impact, fulfilment, abundance, mindset shift, limiting beliefs, step into your power, show up, do the work, level up, transform your life, unlock your potential, embrace your journey, take your business to the next level, achieve your dreams, game-changer, next level, crushing it, hustle, grind, manifest, authentic self, show up fully, lean into, unpack, circle back, bandwidth, synergy, scalable, leverage, pivot.

### 2.10 What removes or rewrites copy (three mechanisms, in order)
1. **Scoring.** Same compliance score as Node 6, plus a type-specific selection score (body copy: +10 for a PDC angle, +10 for 100–170 words, +10 for no banned word; link descriptions: +10 for ≤60 characters, +10 for starting capitalised, +15 for an action verb).
2. **The output gate.** Every piece passes a combined policy-and-fabrication check before storage. A failing piece is **dropped, not stored** — the coach never sees it. Dropping rather than failing the whole run is deliberate (a deck carries 15–30 variants). The gate checks each piece against a corpus of the coach's own supplied material, so an unsupported claim is caught as fabrication.
3. **Capped regeneration.** A dropped piece triggers a redraft, up to three attempts, each re-checked; a redraft that fails again is discarded. **Body copy only** — measured on production, 100% of drops were bodies (a 40-character headline has too little room for a redraft to change the verdict).

## 3. What research the code currently cites (by name)
The copy path cites four named sources, all as source-code comments anchoring specific instructions:

- **[HOOK-MAP]** — *Meta Ads Creative Strategy 2026: Mapping Hook Patterns to Schwartz Awareness Stages*, §2–6. The sole source for which body angle carries which awareness stage.
- **[COHERENCE]** — *Image-and-Copy Coherence: The Matched-Pair Principle*, §2 "The Matched-Pair Awareness Map." It splits each stage into a headline focus (intrigue) and a primary-text focus (invite); the code takes the **primary-text column only**.
- **[PROSPECT]** — *The Definitive B2C Prospecting & Creative Architecture Playbook*, §3, on Unaware copy mechanics. Also the source of the 3/3/1/1/0 cold-stage weighting, whose warmer 25% tail the code preserves as "a vital safeguard against Entity-ID pigeonholing."
- **The Meta compliance reference** (an internal synthesis document), cited for the register standard and personal-attributes rules.

## 4. Retrieval-mechanics footprint (what is present, what is absent)
A full search of the server codebase for the terms *Rosetta, joint embedding, embedding space, language encoder, text encoder, Entity ID, intent scan, linguistic similarity* returns matches **only in the ad-image path** (e.g. "a repeated cell is a repeated Entity ID," "same face under the same lighting collapses to one Entity ID").

**Zero matches** in the headline generator, the ad-copy generator, or the angles file. Specifically confirmed **absent from both copy nodes**:
- No Rosetta / optical-character-recognition logic.
- No embedding, vector, or joint-embedding-space concept.
- No Entity-ID reasoning — nothing checks whether two headlines or two body copies would be treated by Meta as the same ad or different ads.
- No intent-scan or token-window logic — nothing counts, weights, or front-loads the opening tokens.
- No similarity measurement between generated variants of any kind.
- No awareness-encoding in the headline path at all: Node 6 has no stage concept, and Node 7's headlines and link descriptions are stage-blind. Only body copy carries a stage.

**One partial exception**, stated precisely: the Unaware stage-guidance paragraph contains a single instruction derived from retrieval mechanics — *"Lead with the counterintuitive observation, so the first lines carry the specific subject matter rather than a generic opening (the platform reads the opening tokens to classify what this ad is about)"* ([PROSPECT §3]). That sentence is the entire footprint of retrieval-mechanics thinking in the copy path. It is prose guidance to the model, in one of five stage paragraphs, applied to body copy only. **Nothing measures or enforces it.**

## 5. The separate concept engine (not used for published ad copy)
A distinct system generates "concepts" — records combining a persona, a desire, an awareness stage, a named **hook pattern**, a headline, short text and long text — built on **seven named hook patterns** mapped to the five awareness stages, with its own validator and structural rules.

This engine does **not** produce the ad copy described above. The ad-copy generator triggers it in the background but deliberately never waits for it and never reads its output (reading a concept there would be a race condition), so the stage assignment is derived from the shared allocation rule instead. The concept records feed the **video-script generator**. The Meta-facing headlines, body copy and link descriptions a coach actually publishes come entirely from Nodes 6 and 7.
