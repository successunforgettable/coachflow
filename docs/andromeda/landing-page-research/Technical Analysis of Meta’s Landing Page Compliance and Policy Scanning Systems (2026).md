The Scanning Mechanism: Facebookexternalhit Protocol The Facebookexternalhit robot is the primary agent responsible for fetching destination data to generate link previews and perform compliance scans. **Crawling Behavior and Technical Constraints** The crawler focuses on Open Graph (OG) tags (og:title, og:description, og:image, og:url) to interpret page content i. To ensure efficient processing, the crawler adheres to the "first 1MB" rule, reading only the first one million characters of a page i. It is critical that OG tags and safety-related metadata are placed high within the section to ensure capture before this threshold is reached i. **Technical Failure Modes** As a Senior Architect, I identify four primary failure modes that disrupt the integrity of the scan: \* **Slow Page Loading:** If a page fails to load within the crawler’s timeout window, the system generates incomplete previews, often triggering lower Ad Quality scores in the auction i. \* **Cache Problems:** The system often stores stale data. Use the Sharing Debugger to "scrape again" and force a re-fetch of updated landing page assets i. \* **Firewall/Blocking:** Security settings may inadvertently flag the crawler as a malicious agent i. \* **Server Slowdown/Stop:** The bot’s intensity—reading pages too rapidly—can overwhelm hosts, leading to automated blocking and failed compliance checks i. If OG tags are missing, the system attempts to generate information automatically, which frequently results in unprofessional previews and manual review triggers i. \#\#\#\#\# 3\. Compliance Triggers and Review Lifecycle The scanning systems are triggered by both proactive lifecycle events and reactive user-generated signals. **Standard vs. Reactive Triggers** \* **Standard Triggers:** Ad submission and major edits (URL or creative changes) initiate an automated destination scan to verify alignment with the ad's proposition i. \* **Reactive Triggers:** High volumes of negative feedback or user reports initiate secondary deep-scans of the destination URL i. **Targeting Inversion and Signal Density** The modern system utilizes "Targeting Inversion," where computer vision and semantic analysis read the creative and landing page content to predict user relevance i. Under the Andromeda engine, retrieval model complexity has increased 10,000x, transforming the landing page from a simple safety check into a high-density signal for the retrieval engine i. The system now essentially "targets" the audience based on the semantic content of the destination experience rather than relying on advertiser-defined interests i. \#\#\#\#\# 4\. Policy Rejection Criteria: Prohibited Content & Disruptive UX Automated scanners penalize specific elements that degrade user experience or violate community standards. **Categorized Violations** \* **Misleading Claims:** False attributes, non-functional features, and deceptive value propositions that do not align with the ad creative i. \* **Disruptive UX:** Meta penalizes "fatiguing" experiences such as heavy interstitials, pop-ups, or non-functional navigation i. \* **Prohibited Content:** While AI Chat Data (Meta AI interactions) is now a targeting signal, conversations regarding religion, health, politics, or sexual orientation are strictly excluded from this signal i. **Entity ID and Diagnostic Feedback** Andromeda clusters visually similar ads under a single **Entity ID** i. If a landing page associated with an Entity ID is flagged for Disruptive UX, all ads sharing that visual "ticket" will face retrieval suppression i. This is critical given that Creative Quality—including the landing page experience—accounts for 56% of total performance outcomes i. Marketers can monitor these impacts via the **Ad Quality Ranking** (Above Average, Average, or Below Average) within Ads Manager i. \#\#\#\#\# 5\.
