**Replaces the earlier scripts.** Rewritten against the ten documents in `docs/andromeda/script-research/` after grading.

Six scripts: three audiences × two lengths. Record all six in one session.

---

## What the grading found, and what changed

**1 · Pacing was over the ceiling on all three.**

> *"3-Words-Per-Second Limit: This is your absolute maximum speed. Any faster and you lose the natural cadence required for comprehension."*

The v1 scripts ran 3.4–3.8 words per second. Every script below is inside the limit with the buffer applied.

> *"Buffer Rule: Time the script to read 2-3 seconds shy of the final recording time to allow for talent emphasis."*

> *"Without these pauses, the script becomes a 'garbled ear-full' that confuses the listener and destroys conversion."*

**2 · The turn arrived eleven seconds late.**

> *"Once your hook ends at the 3-second mark, you must immediately bridge into the body using an authoritative transition like 'Here's how,' 'Watch this.'"*

V1 put the reframe at the one-third mark. Everything below bridges at three seconds.

**3 · Nothing was withheld.**

> *"the strategist must create an 'open loop' or 'mystery' that the brain feels compelled to resolve"* · *"If they think they've seen your solution before, they will scroll."*

V1 named the four patterns and then explained them. Now they're named and **not** explained — that's the loop the viewer has to click to close.

**4 · No physical anchor.**

> *"scripts often pair spoken curiosity with a 'weird object' visual — such as holding a thumb drive or an unrelated item — to force the viewer to ask, 'What is that and why is it relevant?'"*

Each 50-second script now opens on you holding something.

**5 · The awareness label was wrong, and it changes the length.**

I labelled these "problem-unaware," which isn't a Schwartz stage. Read against the taxonomy: your buyer **feels** the pain — capped salary, nothing building — but doesn't know the answer. That's **Problem-Aware**, not Unaware.

> *"Problem-Aware → 30-60s → This duration allows for the validation of the user's struggle."*

Which makes 30s and 50s both correct, and resolves the Reels-versus-Feed mismatch:

> *"Reels & Stories | 15-30s | 9:16"* · *"Feed | Up to 60s | 1:1 / 4:5"*

Hence two versions of each: a 30-second Reels cut at 9:16, and a 50-second Feed version at 4:5. Two native assets, two Entity IDs, no letterboxing.

**6 · Name-dropping — resolved differently.**

The research prescribes it. I'm not putting Tony Robbins next to a crypto offer, for the reason your own build notes flag. The authority is your own instead, stated plainly: twenty-five years, fifty-two countries. True, checkable, no endorsement risk.

## Delivery rules for every script

**Fragments and contractions.** They're written that way. Don't smooth them out when you read.

> *"the intentional use of sentence fragments… mirrors conversational reality, which humanizes the speaker and facilitates a 'transfer of trust'"*

**Numbers spelled out.** "Twenty-five years," not "25." Prevents stumbling on camera.

**One idea per breath.** If you can't say a line comfortably in one breath, you're rushing it.

**Take the pauses.** The word counts sit below the ceiling specifically to give you room. Silence reads as confidence.

**No made-up contact details.** Say "link below." Never improvise a URL or a phone number.

Counted from the text, not estimated. All six sit under the three-words-per-second ceiling with the buffer applied — comfortably, which means you have room to take the pauses rather than race the clock. If you speak faster than these timings, you have spare seconds rather than a problem.

**Hook lengths** — the research specifies under ten words for the opening segment. All six comply.

**Change something visible between each pair.** Standing versus seated, different angle, different background. Two videos that look the same compete with each other instead of reaching different people.

# Post-production

**Captions burned in.** Sixty-nine percent watch with sound off. Large, high contrast, inside the centre safe band — not the bottom third, where the CTA button sits.

**One super on the turn.** The research calls for on-screen text reinforcing the spoken message:
FOUR PATTERNS. ONE IS YOURS. · YOUR JUDGEMENT DOESN'T TRAVEL. · NOBODY TELLS YOU WHEN YOU'RE READY.

**First frame is your face mid-sentence.** No title card, no logo, no fade.

**Cut hard at the end.** No outro, no music tail.

**Each script points at its own landing page.** Professionals to page one, entrepreneurs to page two, women to page three.

# What the research prescribes that I've deliberately not done

**Name-dropping a celebrity.** You have a genuine Tony Robbins endorsement and the research says to use it. I've used your own credentials instead, because a celebrity name adjacent to a crypto offer reads as endorsing the crypto — the risk already flagged in the page-one build notes. Your call if you want to overrule it.

**The bold guarantee, the "Vegas bet."** Prescribed for hot audiences. It requires promising an outcome, which this campaign cannot do under Meta's financial-claims rules or the UAE's. Deliberately omitted.
