# The Digital Asset Blueprint — Ad Copy Brief

Three audiences. Eighteen concepts. Each with an image hook, primary text and headline.

**Images are specified separately in `image-brief.md`.** This document is copy only — image hooks are listed here so you can see which hook pairs with which body, but every prompt, sub-type, format and safe-zone rule lives in the image brief.

**This is where the targeting actually happens.** The landing pages don't select the audience — the ad creative does. Everything below is built against `Andromeda_Copy_Awareness_Stage_Retrieval.md` and `Andromeda_Copy_Opening_Tokens.md`.

---

## The three rules everything follows

**1. The first ten words decide who sees the ad.**

> *"the first 10 tokens dictate whether the algorithm retrieves the ad, while the first 125 characters dictate whether the user clicks it."*

Opening tokens set the vector that routes the ad into a branch of Meta's index. Everything after them is persuasion for a human who has already been shown the ad.

**2. Category-agnostic pain reaches people outside the category.**

> *"Text focusing on category-agnostic pain points… allows the ad to qualify for retrieval for users whose sequence histories show initial, informational research behavior… even if they have never visited a mattress site."*

> *"Text utilizing analytical, comparative, or ingredient-based hooks… aligns with users whose event logs indicate active market consideration."*

Read across: write about a capped salary and a dormant balance, and you reach the professional who has never bought crypto. Write about protocols, tokens, market cycles or wallets, and you reach the enthusiast. **The repulsion is not a disclaimer you add. It is the vocabulary you decline to use.**

**3. Specific is not the same as category-naming.**

The research warns against conversational openings — *"Hey guys, are you tired of…"* primes the model with noise and routes the ad into broad, irrelevant branches. But high-salience does not mean naming the product. It means density and specificity.

"Eleven years of tax-free income and no pension behind it" is high-salience and category-agnostic. It routes to financial-anxiety and career nodes. "Evaluating Bitcoin versus Ethereum allocation" is high-salience and category-specific — it routes to crypto nodes, which is the audience you do not want.

**Every surface must agree.** Image text, primary text and headline are read as separate inputs and fused. A cold body with a market-savvy headline creates semantic noise and lowers retrieval priority. Keep all three in the same awareness stage.

---

## Weighting

Per the 50/30/20 matrix, and because a free webinar has no discount play:

- **Four problem-unaware per audience** — the reach engine. No category vocabulary anywhere in the first ten words.
- **Two solution-aware per audience** — for people already asking how to build something, but not yet shopping crypto.
- **No product-aware or transactional ads.** Discount-hook copy would route to deep-funnel nodes and starve the account of reach.

# Writing checklist

For any new ad written against this brief:

- [ ] First ten words contain no crypto or category vocabulary
- [ ] First ten words name a pain, not a product
- [ ] No conversational filler opening ("Hey guys", "Are you tired of")
- [ ] Headline 40 characters or fewer
- [ ] Image hook, headline and body all in the same awareness stage
- [ ] No comparison framing, no technical vocabulary, in the first 125 characters
- [ ] No number, timeframe, return, or income claim on any surface
- [ ] Nothing promised in the ad that isn't visible and true on the landing page
- [ ] This concept is visually and semantically distinct from the other five
