# Shooting Guide — Digital Asset Blueprint Video Ads

**Six videos, one session, roughly two hours including resets.**

Everything here is for a phone. Do not hire a studio. The research is explicit that raw outperforms polished in this format — a slightly imperfect frame reads as a real person talking, a produced one reads as an advertisement and gets scrolled.

Read this alongside `video-ad-scripts.md`. This guide covers how to shoot; that file covers what to say.

---

## Before you start — the things that ruin a shoot

**Shoot each ratio natively.** This is the single most important instruction in this document. Three videos are 9:16 vertical and three are 4:5. **Do not shoot everything vertical and crop later.** Non-native assets get de-prioritised by Meta, and near-identical crops of the same footage collapse into one Entity ID, which means they compete with each other instead of reaching different people.

On iPhone: 9:16 is the standard vertical video mode. For 4:5, shoot vertical at the highest quality and frame with generous headroom and side space so the editor can crop to 4:5 without losing you. Tell your editor which is which.

**Turn the phone sideways in your head, not in your hand.** All six are vertical or near-vertical. Nothing is horizontal.

**4K, 30fps.** Not 60. Not 24. Thirty is what the platform expects and it keeps file sizes sane.

**Lock exposure and focus** before each take. Tap and hold on your face until the lock indicator appears. Otherwise the phone will hunt for focus mid-sentence and you'll lose the take.

**Airplane mode.** A notification vibration ruins audio.

**Clean the lens.** Genuinely. Phone lenses are always smeared and it costs you sharpness.

---

## Audio — the thing that decides whether these work

Bad audio kills a video faster than bad picture. People forgive a soft image; they don't forgive echo.

**Use a lavalier mic** clipped inside your shirt about a hand's width below your chin, or wired earbuds with a mic. The phone's built-in mic is acceptable only if you're within arm's length and the room is soft.

**Test the room by clapping once.** If you hear a ring afterwards, the room is too hard. Move somewhere with a rug, curtains, a sofa, or soft furnishings. Kitchens and glass-walled offices are the worst rooms in most homes.

**Record a few seconds of silence** at the start of each session. Your editor needs it to remove room tone.

**Air conditioning off.** It's inaudible to you and very audible on the recording.

## Lighting — one rule, then the detail

**Face the light. Never sit with your back to a window.**

That single rule fixes ninety percent of amateur video. Everything below is refinement.

### Daytime setups (videos 3, 4)

Stand or sit facing a window, roughly two metres back from it, with the window slightly off to one side rather than dead in front. Straight-on window light is flat; light coming from about 45 degrees gives your face shape.

Sheer curtain across the window if the sun is direct. Direct sunlight on your face creates hard shadows and makes you squint.

**Nothing brighter behind you than in front of you.** If there's a second window behind you, close the blind.

### Evening setups (videos 1, 2, 5, 6)

Evening looks better than daytime for these and it's easier to control, because you're not fighting changing sun.

**Key light:** one soft source in front of you and slightly to one side, at roughly head height or a touch above. A ring light, a softbox, or a lamp with the shade on bounced off a white wall. Warm rather than blue — around 3000K if your light lets you choose.

**Do not put the light directly behind the camera.** That's the flat, interrogated look. Move it thirty to forty-five degrees to one side.

**Background separation:** a second small lamp behind you and out of frame, pointed at the wall, stops you merging into a dark background. A practical lamp already in the room works.

**Let the background go darker than your face.** Evening ads read as intimate and confessional, which suits the material.

## The six setups

### Video 1 — Professionals, 30 seconds, 9:16 vertical

**Setting:** Apartment, evening. City visible through a window behind you if you have it, thrown out of focus.

**Framing:** Standing. Chest up. Your eyes at roughly the top third of the frame. Leave clear space above your head and to one side — captions go there.

**Camera height:** Eye level. Not below you, ever — phone-on-a-table looking up is the least authoritative angle there is.

**Distance:** Arm's length plus a little. Close enough to feel like you're talking to one person.

**Lighting:** Warm key from one side. Background darker than your face, city lights soft behind.

**Delivery note:** You're standing, which naturally raises energy. Good for the shortest cut.

---

### Video 2 — Professionals, 50 seconds, 4:5

**Setting:** Same apartment, seated at a table or counter. Change the angle from video 1 so the two don't look like the same shot.

**Prop:** Your passport. On the table in front of you before you start. Pick it up on the first line, hold it up for about two seconds, then set it down. Don't wave it.

**Framing:** Seated, chest up, with the table edge just visible in the bottom of frame so the passport has somewhere to land. Slightly wider than video 1 because the object needs room.

**Camera height:** Eye level with you seated. Prop the phone on books if you have to.

**Lighting:** Warm key from one side, background falling off into darkness. This is the most intimate of the six — let it look like evening.

**Delivery note:** Seated and slower than video 1. Take the pause after "One of them is yours" properly — count two beats before moving on.

---

### Video 3 — Entrepreneurs, 30 seconds, 9:16 vertical

**Setting:** Your office or workspace, daytime. Something recognisably a place of work behind you, blurred.

**Framing:** Standing. Chest up, same as video 1 but a different room and a different backdrop.

**Lighting:** Facing a window, off to one side. Daylight. Brighter and harder than the apartment videos, which is right — this one is about work, not reflection.

**Delivery note:** Direct and slightly challenging. This audience responds to being told something true, not asked a question.

---

### Video 4 — Entrepreneurs, 50 seconds, 4:5

**Setting:** Same office, seated at your desk. Wider than video 3.

**Prop:** A single key or a small set of keys. Hold it up on the first line, then set it down on the desk with a small deliberate sound. That sound is worth keeping — the editor should not cut it.

**Framing:** Seated, desk edge visible in the lower frame. Chest up with enough room for the hand holding the key.

**Camera height:** Eye level seated.

**Lighting:** Window light from the side, softened by a curtain if direct. Keep a lamp on in the background for depth.

**Delivery note:** Lean in slightly on "your judgement doesn't travel." That's the line the whole ad turns on.

---

### Video 5 — Women, 30 seconds, 9:16 vertical

**Setting:** A softer room. Living room, a warmer corner, not an office. Evening or late afternoon.

**Framing:** Seated. Chest up. Slightly closer than the other 30-second cuts.

**Camera height:** Eye level seated.

**Lighting:** Warm, soft, low contrast. Less dramatic than the professionals videos — the shadows should be gentle rather than moody.

**Delivery note:** Warm and level. Not sympathetic, not gentle. This audience is competent and the delivery has to respect that. Say it the way you'd say it to a colleague, not the way you'd say it to someone who needs looking after.

---

### Video 6 — Women, 50 seconds, 4:5

**Setting:** Same room, closer framing, different angle from video 5.

**Prop:** A folded notebook. Hold it up on the first line, open it as you move into the bridge.

**Framing:** Seated, closer than video 5, with room in the lower frame for the notebook.

**Lighting:** The warmest and softest of the six. Low contrast.

**Delivery note:** The turn — "That's not a knowledge gap. It's a pattern" — is the most important passage in all six videos. Slow down, hold eye contact, and don't soften it.

## Getting the take

**Start recording, then start talking after two seconds of silence.** Those two seconds are handles for the editor. The final video starts on you mid-sentence, so record more than you need at the front.

**Don't say "hi" or "hey guys" or introduce yourself.** The research names conversational filler openings as a failure mode — it primes the algorithm with noise and routes the ad into broad, irrelevant branches. Go straight into the hook.

**Three takes minimum per script.** The third is almost always the best — the first is stiff, the second is over-corrected, the third is you.

**If you fluff a line, keep going.** The recovery often reads as more real than a clean take. Only restart if you lose the thread entirely.

**Look at the lens, not at yourself.** Every phone tempts you to watch your own face. If it helps, put a small sticker beside the lens and talk to that.

**Between each pair, change something visible.** Stand for one, sit for the next. Move the camera. Change the background. Two videos that look alike get clustered by Meta and compete with each other instead of reaching different people.

## Session order

Shoot in this order. It groups by location so you're not relighting six times.

| order | video | location | ratio | prop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Video 3 — Entrepreneurs 30s | Office, daytime | 9:16 | — |
| 2 | Video 4 — Entrepreneurs 50s | Office, daytime | 4:5 | Key |
| 3 | Video 5 — Women 30s | Soft room, afternoon | 9:16 | — |
| 4 | Video 6 — Women 50s | Soft room, afternoon | 4:5 | Notebook |
| 5 | Video 1 — Professionals 30s | Apartment, evening | 9:16 | — |
| 6 | Video 2 — Professionals 50s | Apartment, evening | 4:5 | Passport |

Daytime setups first, evening last, so you're shooting each in its natural light.

**Same shirt throughout** is fine and saves time. The variation Meta reads is in framing, background and composition, not wardrobe.

## Before you stop shooting

- [ ] Three usable takes of all six scripts
- [ ] Videos 1, 3, 5 shot natively vertical 9:16
- [ ] Videos 2, 4, 6 framed with room to crop to 4:5
- [ ] All three props appear and are handled without waving
- [ ] Room tone recorded — a few seconds of silence
- [ ] Audio checked on headphones, not the phone speaker
- [ ] No take starts with "hi," "hey," or "so"
- [ ] Each pair looks visibly different from the other
