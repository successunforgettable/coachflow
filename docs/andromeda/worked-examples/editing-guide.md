# Editing Guide — Digital Asset Blueprint Video Ads

**Six videos. Three audiences, two lengths each.**

These are Meta ads, not content. Almost every instruction below exists because of how Meta's delivery system reads video, not because of taste. Where a rule has a reason worth knowing, it's stated.

| 1 | `professionals-30-reels.mp4` | 28–30s | 9:16 | Reels & Stories |
| 2 | `professionals-50-feed.mp4` | 47–50s | 4:5 | Feed |

| 6 | `women-50-feed.mp4` | 47–50s | 4:5 | Feed |

**Do not cross-crop.** The 9:16 videos were shot vertical and stay vertical. The 4:5 videos are cropped from vertical footage to 4:5 and stay 4:5. Never export a 4:5 version of a 9:16 video or vice versa. Meta clusters near-identical assets under one identifier and they end up competing with each other rather than reaching different audiences.

## Export specification

| setting | value |
|---|---|
| Resolution | 1080 × 1920 (9:16) · 1080 × 1350 (4:5) |
| Frame rate | 30fps, matching the source |
| Codec | H.264 |
| Audio | AAC, 128kbps or higher, stereo |
| Colour | sRGB |
| File size | Under 100MB per video |
| Loudness | Normalise dialogue to around −14 LUFS |

---

## The three rules that matter most

**1 · The first frame is his face, mid-sentence.**

No title card. No logo. No fade in. No black frame. No two seconds of him settling into position.

Meta scores the first three seconds separately from the rest of the video, and that score decides whether the ad gets retrieved at all. Cut into the take after he's already started speaking — if the raw file has him drawing breath before the first word, trim it out.

**2 · Captions are burned in, and they are not optional.**

Sixty-nine percent of viewers watch with the sound off. A video that only works with audio loses two thirds of its audience before it starts.

Burn them into the picture. Do not rely on platform auto-captions — they render inconsistently and can be switched off.

**3 · Cut hard at the end.**

No outro. No music tail. No logo sting. No fade to black. The last spoken word, a beat of silence, cut.

---

## Caption specification

**Position: upper-middle of the frame.** Not the bottom third — that's where Meta's own CTA button, caption text and profile row sit, and anything you put there gets covered.

On a 1080 × 1920 frame, keep all text between **250px and 1248px** vertically. That band is the only area guaranteed visible across placements.

**Style:**
- Bold sans-serif. Something plain — Inter, Helvetica, Montserrat. No script fonts, no outlines-plus-shadows-plus-glow.
- White text with a subtle dark shadow, or white text on a semi-transparent dark bar. Enough contrast to read against any frame.
- Large. Roughly 5–7% of frame height per line.
- **Two to four words per caption card.** Not full sentences. They should change at the rhythm of speech.
- Sentence case or all caps, but pick one and keep it across all six.

**Timing:** captions appear as the word is spoken, not before. Karaoke-style word highlighting is unnecessary — clean card changes are enough.

## The supers — one per video

Separate from the captions. Each video gets a single emphasis card on its turn — the line the whole ad pivots on. Larger than the captions, held for two to three seconds, then gone.

| video | super | appears on |
|---|---|---|
| Professionals 30s | FOUR PATTERNS. ONE IS YOURS. | "There are four reasons…" |
| Professionals 50s | FOUR PATTERNS. ONE IS YOURS. | "There are four of them." |
| Entrepreneurs 30s | YOUR JUDGEMENT DOESN'T TRAVEL | on that line |
| Entrepreneurs 50s | YOUR JUDGEMENT DOESN'T TRAVEL | on that line |
| Women 30s | NOBODY TELLS YOU WHEN | "waiting to feel qualified" |
| Women 50s | IT'S OLDER THAN THE MONEY | on that line |

Same position rule — inside the safe band, never the bottom third.

## Per-video edit notes

### Video 1 — Professionals 30s

Straight cut, no inserts. Target 28 seconds.

**The one thing to protect:** the pause after "One of them is yours." Two full beats of silence before the CTA. Do not tighten it — that silence is doing the work of the whole ad.

### Video 2 — Professionals 50s

**The passport.** He picks it up on the first line and sets it down. Hold that shot — don't cut away from it. The object is there to make the viewer ask what it is, and cutting early closes the loop before it opens.

Target 47 seconds. Protect the pause after "One of them is yours."

### Video 3 — Entrepreneurs 30s

Fastest of the six. Target 28 seconds. No pauses to protect — this one runs straight through.

### Video 4 — Entrepreneurs 50s

**The key.** He sets it down on the desk and it makes a small sound. **Keep that sound.** Do not clean it out with the room tone. It's a real moment and it lands.

Target 47 seconds. Give "your judgement doesn't travel" a beat of air after it.

### Video 5 — Women 30s

Softest grade of the six. Target 28 seconds.

### Video 6 — Women 50s

**The notebook** opens as he moves into the bridge. Let that movement play.

Target 48 seconds. **The turn — "That's not a knowledge gap. It's a pattern. And it's usually older than the money" — is the most important passage across all six videos.** Give it room. If anything needs trimming to hit length, trim the CTA, not this.

## Music and sound

**No music under the dialogue.** These are one person talking to one person. Music makes it an advertisement, and the whole design is that it shouldn't feel like one.

**Remove room tone** using the silence he recorded at the start of each session.

**Keep the natural sounds** that come from the props — the key on the desk, the notebook opening. Those are texture, not noise.

**Normalise dialogue to about −14 LUFS** so all six sit at the same level and none of them jumps out when someone scrolls past.

---

## What not to do

**No jump cuts every two seconds.** That's a content-creator style and it reads as content. These should feel like one continuous person talking. Cut only to remove a fluff or tighten a gap.

**No zoom punch-ins.** Same reason.

**No stock B-roll.** Nothing of trading screens, charts, city skylines or people shaking hands. Aside from looking generic, chart and screen imagery signals the wrong audience to Meta — these ads are built to reach people who have never touched crypto, and crypto visuals undo that.

**No emoji, no arrows, no animated stickers.**

**No countdown timers or "limited spots" graphics.** Meta names invented scarcity as a misleading-claim trigger.

**No numbers on screen that aren't in the script.** No percentages, no amounts, no returns. If it isn't spoken, it doesn't appear.

---

## Delivery checklist

Per video:

- [ ] First frame is his face, mid-sentence — no card, no fade
- [ ] Captions burned in, upper-middle, inside the safe band
- [ ] One super on the turn, held two to three seconds
- [ ] No music under dialogue
- [ ] Room tone removed, prop sounds kept
- [ ] Dialogue normalised to around −14 LUFS
- [ ] Hard cut at the end, no tail
- [ ] Correct ratio, exported natively, not cropped from the other version
- [ ] Length inside target
- [ ] Nothing on screen claiming money, returns or scarcity

Across the set:

- [ ] Six files, correctly named
- [ ] The two videos in each pair look visibly different from each other
- [ ] Same caption style across all six
