# Production Brief — Nine Ad Videos
## Locations, wardrobe, props and delivery

Companion to the script file and the camera brief. This one covers what the room looks like, what you wear, and how each script should sound.

---

# THE THREE BLOCKS

Shoot by block, not by audience. Each block is one location and one set-up session.

| Block | Location | Videos | Time of day |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Office or desk space | P1, E1, W1 | Morning — needs controllable light |
| **2** | Home — kitchen and living room | P2, E2, W2 | Midday to early afternoon |
| **3** | Outdoors | P3, W3, E3 | Late afternoon, E3 last |

**Block 3 is daylight-dependent.** E3 needs late light with real shadows. If the light goes, E3 moves to another day rather than being shot flat.

# BLOCK 1 — OFFICE / DESK

## The room

A working office, not a studio and not a boardroom. What should be visible behind you: a wall, a shelf with real objects on it, a window if there is one. Depth is good — a background six to ten feet behind you rather than a wall you're pressed against.

**What must not be in shot:** branded backdrops, logos, roll-up banners, motivational quotes on the wall, a bookshelf arranged for camera, anything that looks staged. A cold viewer reads staging as advertising.

**Location options in order of preference:** your own office; a quiet corner of a co-working space out of hours; a hotel business lounge before 9am.

## Wardrobe — Block 1

**Base:** collared shirt, no tie, no jacket. Sleeves down or rolled once, not twice.

**Colours:** mid-to-dark solids. Navy, charcoal, deep olive, slate. No white — it blows out under high-key light. No black — it flattens.

**Avoid:** stripes, checks, fine patterns of any kind. They strobe on camera. No visible logos. No lapel pin.

**Three different shirts across P1, E1 and W1.** Different colours, not the same shirt in a different light.

**Watch:** simple, dark strap or steel. It will be visible in E1 and it should look like something you actually wear, not something borrowed.

## Grooming

Matte face — a wipe before each take. Hair as you'd wear it to a meeting. If you wear glasses, wear them consistently across the whole block; do not put them on for one video and remove them for another.

---

## P1 — Professionals · FULL FACE · High-key

**Set-up:** seated or standing at the desk, framed chest-up. Bright even light from the front, minimal shadow on the face. Window light plus a fill.

**Wardrobe:** navy or slate shirt.

**Props:** none needed in frame. Desk can be a working desk.

**Tonality:** measured, and slightly surprised by your own finding. You are reporting something counterintuitive, not selling it. The line *"the most careful people I meet have built the least"* should land as an observation you didn't expect, not as a gotcha. No urgency. No lift at the end of sentences.

**Pace:** slowest of the nine. This one carries the most weight and should not be rushed.

---

## E1 — Entrepreneurs · HANDS ONLY · Natural light

**Set-up:** camera looking down at the desk from slightly above and to one side. Your hands, a laptop, then a stack of invoices, then the laptop closing. **Your face never enters frame — not a shoulder, not a chin.**

**Wardrobe:** what shows is cuffs, forearms and watch. Roll the sleeves once so the forearm reads. Different shirt colour from P1.

**Props needed:** a laptop that looks used. A real stack of paper — invoices, statements, anything with the look of business paperwork. Do not use blank paper; it reads as fake even out of focus.

**Tonality:** direct and businesslike. No warmth needed here. This is one owner talking to another about a thing they both know. The pause after *"ask what they hold outside the company and they go quiet"* is the whole beat — leave it.

**Movement:** hands should be doing something real and unhurried. Closing the laptop lands on the line about the quarter.

---

## W1 — Women · WRITING · Low-key

**Set-up:** a whiteboard or a large pad. You writing four short numbered lines while speaking. Face partly in frame — edge, three-quarter, or turned toward the board. Directional light from one side, genuine shadow on the other.

**Wardrobe:** third shirt colour. Darker works well against low-key.

**Props:** a board or pad and a marker that writes cleanly. **The four lines you write must NOT be the words you are speaking** — write four short numbered items, unlabelled. The frame and the script each carry something the other doesn't.

**Tonality:** the most careful of the nine. Respectful, never pitying, never explaining-to. You are describing something you've observed in capable people, not diagnosing a weakness. The line *"she's run it carefully for years without it once falling over"* is a compliment and must sound like one. If any line reads as condescending, change it on the day.

**Pace:** unhurried. The writing sets the rhythm.

# BLOCK 2 — HOME

## The rooms

**Kitchen or dining table** for P2 and E2. **Living room** for W2.

Real domestic space, lived in. Visible: a worktop with things on it, a chair, a lamp, a window. Not styled, not cleared.

**What must not be in shot:** anything expensive on display, a view that reads as luxury, art that dominates, anything that says "this person doesn't have your problem." The whole point of these three is proximity.

## Wardrobe — Block 2

**Base:** no collar needed. Fine-knit jumper, henley, casual shirt open at the neck.

**Colours:** warmer and softer than Block 1 — oatmeal, grey, muted green, faded navy.

**Avoid:** anything that reads as office. No jacket, no formal shirt, no watch that shouts.

**Different from Block 1 and different from each other** across P2, E2 and W2.

---

## P2 — Professionals · HANDS ONLY · Low-key

**Set-up:** camera down onto a kitchen or side table. Your hands, a phone showing a banking app, a coffee going cold beside it. Directional light — one side lit, deep shadow the other. **Face never in frame.**

**Wardrobe:** what shows is sleeves and hands. Soft knit cuff works better here than a shirt cuff.

**Props:** a phone with a plausible banking screen — **blur, crop or cover the actual numbers**, or use a screenshot with the balance obscured. A real mug with real coffee in it, going cold, not steaming.

**Tonality:** the quietest of the nine. Almost thinking aloud. Low energy on purpose — this one works because it sounds like someone noticing something rather than making a point. *"Quietly buying a little less every year that passes"* should be said flatly, not sadly.

---

## E2 — Entrepreneurs · DRAWING · High-key

**Set-up:** camera down onto a table with paper. You drawing two boxes — one large, then a second small one beside it. Face partly visible at the top of frame. Bright, flat, shadowless light.

**Wardrobe:** casual, second Block 2 item.

**Props:** plain A4 or A3 paper, a pen that shows. Draw the large box early and **draw the second box on the line about the second box** — the picture and the words land together.

**Tonality:** teacherly and patient. You are explaining a structure, and the drawing is the explanation. Warmer than E1. The line *"almost nobody gets round to it"* is said without judgement — it is a fact about people, including people who are good at business.

---

## W2 — Women · FULL FACE · Natural light

**Set-up:** seated in the living room, framed chest-up. Warm daylight from a window or open shade. Softest look of the nine.

**Wardrobe:** third Block 2 item, softest of the three.

**Props:** none. Nothing in your hands.

**Tonality:** warm and direct. You are recounting something that happened in a room — the answer you got when you asked. Conversational, not performed. *"No question's too basic"* at the end must sound like a rule you actually enforce, not a nicety.

**The one to watch:** this is the script most at risk of sounding like a pitch, because it's full face with nothing else in shot. Keep the energy down.

# BLOCK 3 — OUTDOORS

## The locations

Three separate outdoor spots, not three angles of one.

- **P3** — a table outdoors. A terrace, a courtyard, a quiet café before it opens. You need a surface to write on.
- **W3** — anywhere with a bench, wall or ledge to rest a notebook on. Bright.
- **E3** — open, wide, with distance behind you. A rooftop, a promenade, a park, a quiet street. **Not a desk, not a table, not a wall behind your head.**

## 🔴 THE ONE THING THAT CANNOT BE FIXED LATER

**All three need a dark or plain low-detail area in frame for the text card to sit on.**

Bokeh, deep shadow, a plain wall, a flat surface. Anything except bright sky or busy sunlit background.

Every video gets a small text line burned in afterwards. Meta reads it with OCR. White text over a bright background cannot be read, the ad loses its category signal, **and nothing anywhere reports the failure.** It is a check that cannot fail.

**W3 especially** — bright outdoor shot with hands in frame. Find the shadow before you roll.

## Wardrobe — Block 3

**Base:** light layers. Linen or cotton shirt, open collar. A light overshirt works for E3 if there's a breeze.

**Colours:** lighter than the other blocks — stone, pale blue, off-white is fine outdoors where it won't blow out.

**Avoid:** sunglasses in E3. The face has to read. Nothing that flaps in wind near the microphone.

**Sound:** outdoors is where audio goes wrong. Lapel mic, and check for wind, traffic and air conditioning units before each take.

---

## P3 — Professionals · WRITING · Natural light

**Set-up:** outdoors at a table. A notebook. You writing a short numbered list as you speak. Face at the edge of frame or turned away. Warm daylight, not direct sun.

**Wardrobe:** linen or cotton shirt, stone or pale blue.

**Props:** a notebook with something already written in it, and a pen. **Use a different notebook from W3.**

**Tonality:** practical and problem-solving. This is the most *useful* of the nine — you are describing a method, not a feeling. Slightly brisker than P1. *"The decision stops depending on whoever you read last"* is the payoff line and should be said plainly.

---

## W3 — Women · HANDS ONLY · Bright

**Set-up:** outdoors, bright. Hands turning the pages of a notebook, then closing it. **Face never in frame.** Find the shadow for the text card.

**Wardrobe:** cuffs and hands only. Light sleeve.

**Props:** a notebook that has genuinely been written in. **A different one from P3.**

**Tonality:** gentle but completely firm on one line. *"After long enough a perfectly capable person starts thinking the problem must be her. It isn't."* — the "It isn't" is the whole script. Say it as a correction of fact, not as reassurance. No softness on that line.

---

## E3 — Entrepreneurs · FULL FACE · Late light, wide

**Set-up:** wide shot, considerable space around you. Late afternoon light with real shadows. **Shoot this last** — it needs the light.

**Wardrobe:** lightest of the three. Open collar, no sunglasses.

**Props:** none.

**Tonality:** level and unsentimental. Peer to peer. This is the most direct of the nine and the shortest hook — four words. *"You'd want the numbers. The downside. What happens if you're wrong."* — three separate statements, three separate breaths, no rush between them.

**Do not smile through this one.** It works because it sounds like someone being straight with you.

# ACROSS ALL NINE

**Delivery register:** you are a person who has noticed something, talking to someone who hasn't. Not a coach, not a speaker, not on stage. If any take sounds like a stage voice, do it again.

**Energy:** lower than feels right. Cold audiences read high energy as advertising. The scripts carry the point; the delivery should get out of the way.

**Sentence endings:** flat, not lifted. A rising end sounds like a sales line.

**The pauses are written in.** Every full stop is a place to stop. The scripts are short so the pauses fit — if you fill them you will finish in 35 seconds and it will sound rushed.

**Read each script aloud before shooting it.** If you stumble, the line is written rather than spoken. Change it on the day.

# PROPS CHECKLIST

- Laptop, used-looking — **E1**
- Stack of real business paperwork — **E1**
- Phone with banking screen, numbers obscured — **P2**
- Mug of cold coffee — **P2**
- A4 or A3 plain paper and a pen that shows — **E2**
- Whiteboard or large pad and a clean marker — **W1**
- Two different notebooks, both already written in — **P3** and **W3** (do not reuse the same one)
- Nine wardrobe items, three per block, all different
- Lapel mic and wind protection for Block 3
