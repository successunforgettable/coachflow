### Comprehensive Report on Video Ad Script Structure and Timing Metrics

#### 1\. Introduction to Strategic Scriptwriting

In high-stakes video production, a structured script is the essential blueprint for message clarity and audience retention 7\. However, as script production leads, we must distinguish between writing for 'the eye' and writing for 'the ear'. Spoken scripts must bypass the formal constraints of prose to sound natural and conversational, often utilising sentence fragments to mirror authentic speech patterns 7.The overarching strategic objective is to sell a 'micro-commitment'—the click—rather than the entire product or service immediately 6\. Over-explaining at the ad stage is a tactical error; the script’s primary function is to propel the viewer toward the sales copy or landing page, where the deeper conversion and actual sale occur 6\.

#### 2\. Functional Anatomy of a High-Converting Script

A high-converting script is meticulously engineered into five psychological segments:

* **The Hook:**  This must secure attention within the first 3 seconds 4\. Effective tactics include bold statements, direct questions, or mysterious visual cues—such as holding a physical object like a thumb drive—to create an 'open loop' that the viewer feels compelled to close 4, 6\.  
* **The Problem:**  Here, we identify a specific pain point or challenge, building the necessary tension to make the audience receptive to a remedy 4, 6\.  
* **The Turn:**  This is the critical shift from problem to solution. Strategically, we frame the solution as a  **"new opportunity"**  or a unique  **"loophole/algorithm"**  that the viewer hasn't encountered before, effectively bypassing market scepticism 6\.  
* **The Solution:**  The product or tip is introduced as the resolution to the tension. We must prioritise benefits over features 4, 6\. At this stage, we employ  **"Name Dropping"**  to build authority rapport; mentioning reputable figures like Tony Robbins or Dean Graziosi helps establish immediate credibility 6\.  
* **The Call to Action (CTA):**  This guides the viewer toward the next step, such as "Click the link" or "Comment below" 4, 6\.

#### 3\. Standard Word Counts and Timing Benchmarks

To avoid a "garbled ear-full" that confuses the listener, scripts must maintain a natural cadence 5\. Industry pacing typically allows for approximately 3 words per second 8\.

Scripts should be timed to finish 2–3 seconds short of the final duration (e.g., a 27-second script for a 30-second spot). This provides essential "breathing room" for the voice talent to emphasise key points and for the audience to process information 5, 8\.

#### 4\. Segmented Breakdown by Video Length

We apply specific structural maps to ensure consistent engagement across different ad durations:

* **15-Second Spot:**  High-impact brevity.  
* **Hook:**  3 seconds (approx. 9 words) 4, 8\.  
* **Value/Body:**  9 seconds (approx. 27 words) 4, 8\.  
* **CTA:**  3 seconds (approx. 9 words) 4, 8\.  
* **30-Second Spot:**  The industry standard.  
* **Act Formula:**  A 3s Hook, 12s Value/Body, and 15s CTA/Outro 4\.  
* **Breathing Room Model:**  Aim for the script to conclude at 25–29 seconds to allow for B-roll transitions or musical flourishes 8\.  
* **60-Second Spot:**  Expanded narrative depth.  
* **Hook:**  5 seconds (approx. 15 words) 4, 8\.  
* **Value & Solution:**  45 seconds (approx. 135 words). This allows for deeper problem analysis, authority building, and testimonials 6, 8\.  
* **CTA:**  10 seconds (approx. 30 words) to reiterate benefits and the required action 4, 8\.

#### 5\. Stylistic Execution and 'Plain Language' Integration

To ensure the script is accessible and persuasive, we must prioritise clear writing and conversational flow.

##### Scriptwriter’s Checklist

*   **Use personal pronouns:**  Speak directly to the viewer using "you" and "your" 2\.  
*   **Prioritise short sentences:**  Contain only one idea per sentence to aid comprehension 2, 7\.  
*   **Round off numbers:**  Use "around 6,000" instead of "6,049" for easier ear-processing 7\.  
*   **Employ active voice:**  Use direct commands (e.g., "Save time today") to drive momentum 2, 4\.  
*   **Avoid jargon:**  Replace corporate-speak with common words familiar to the audience 2, 7\.  
*   **The "Ear Test":**  Read the script aloud to ensure it flows naturally as human speech 4, 7\.  
*   **Audience Testing:**  Present the script to a representative of the target audience to ensure zero jargon-drift and that the main message is clear 2\.

#### 6\. Analysis of Source Disagreements and Signals

Strategic leads must evaluate word density based on the final medium:

* **Word Count Context:**  Source 5 (Zizzo) recommends lower word counts (150–170 for 60s), while Source 8 (JL Recording) allows for higher density (up to 180). This discrepancy is contextual: Zizzo’s conservative counts are safer for  **Radio**  (where sound is the only context), whereas higher counts are acceptable for  **Video** , as on-screen text supports the ear 5, 8\.  
* **Duration of 'The Turn':**  There is "no strong signal" regarding the exact duration of this segment 6\. It is functionally a bridge between the problem and the solution; its length depends on the complexity of the "new opportunity" being presented.

#### 7\. Technical Production Elements

A professional script integrates visual and audio cues to assist the production team:

* **Two-Column Format:**  The left column contains video commands (A-roll, B-roll, shots), while the right column contains all audio/narration 7\.  
* **Natural Sound (Nat Sound):**  Incorporate environmental audio (e.g., barnyard noises or office hum) to indicate topic changes and increase immersion 7\.  
* **Supers and Lower Thirds:**  Use on-screen text (supers) to reinforce lists, numbers, or name identification. This is vital for assisting visual learners and reinforcing the spoken message 7\.
