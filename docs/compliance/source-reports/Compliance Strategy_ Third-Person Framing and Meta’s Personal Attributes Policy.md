### Compliance Strategy: Third-Person Framing and Meta’s Personal Attributes Policy

#### 1\. Foundational Analysis: Meta’s Personal Attributes Standard

Meta’s Advertising Standards maintain a strict prohibition against ad content that asserts or implies that the advertiser possesses knowledge of a user’s specific personal characteristics. Under the 2026 technical expansion of the Personal Attributes Policy, Meta’s internal review systems have transitioned from reactive enforcement to  **proactive enforcement** , utilizing advanced  **semantic intent detection**  to identify and flag prohibited content before the first impression is served.The core prohibition remains: ads must not reference a viewer’s race, ethnicity, religion, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health status, financial situation, or legal history. However, the "Second-Person Assertion" trap has evolved. Meta now flags  **indirect "you" framing**  and  **symptom-based proxies** . For instance, phrases like "Tired of waking up at 3am?" are increasingly flagged as proxies for protected sleep or mental health conditions. Meta’s automated review is now  **multimodal** , scanning not only body copy but also image text via Optical Character Recognition (OCR), video frames, and audio files to detect inferred attributes or disability status.

#### 2\. The Mechanics of Third-Person Case-Study Framing

Third-person framing is a strategic linguistic shift where the advertiser moves from direct address ("you") to neutral, narrative-based, or benefit-focused messaging. This approach serves as a compliance shield by reframing the ad from an "identity-based" assertion to an "interest-based" or "aspirational" offer.The transition requires four specific linguistic shifts to navigate Meta's semantic classifiers:

1. **Identity to Intent:**  Shift from who the person  *is*  to what they are  *looking for*  (e.g., from "Are you a senior?" to "For those exploring retirement options").  
2. **Diagnosis to Resource:**  Pivot from stating a condition to offering a tool (e.g., from "Struggling with depression?" to "Support for people seeking calmer days").  
3. **Individual to Community:**  Frame the service as part of a broader community or demographic (e.g., from "Your bad credit" to "Financial solutions for diverse backgrounds").  
4. **Possessive to Neutral:**  Remove possessive adjectives when referencing symptoms or traits (e.g., from "Your acne" to "A routine for clearer-looking skin").

#### 5\. Industry-Specific Risk Mitigation

##### Health & Wellness

The distinction in visual framing is critical. While Meta restricts side-by-side "before and after" photos for weight loss and anti-aging treatments to prevent  **negative self-perception** , general cosmetics are permitted to show transformations if they avoid shaming tactics. Advertisers should also distinguish between prohibited "Nicotine Pouches" and "Caffeine Pouches"; the latter are generally allowed but reviewed case-by-case to avoid  **unsubstantiated clinical assertions** .

##### Financial Services

Advertisers must lead with educational value rather than income promises. Avoid addressing debt or bankruptcy directly, reframing these as "financial options." Furthermore, Meta’s internal review system scans for  **landing page "dark patterns"** —such as advertising "Free Shipping" while hiding a minimum purchase requirement. For EU campaigns, standard financial risk warnings must meet specific font size requirements (12pt static/28px video).

##### Crypto & Web3

Beyond requiring prior written permission, advertisers must navigate geographic "hard nos." Even with permission, cryptocurrency and DeFi products are excluded from targeting regions including  **Belgium, Greece, Qatar, Russia, Singapore, Slovenia, and Ukraine** . Copy should focus on "blockchain innovation" rather than speculative gains to avoid being flagged as deceptive marketing.

#### 6\. The Strategy Verdict: Does Third-Person Framing Avoid Violations?

Third-person framing is the primary strategic recommendation to avoid Personal Attribute violations. By utilizing narrative and benefit-focused copy, advertisers successfully bypass direct-address triggers. However, the ad remains subject to the full breadth of Meta’s standards.A third-person ad can still be rejected for  **Misleading Claims**  if it promises unrealistic outcomes or for  **Prohibited Content**  if it uses offensive language. The strategy’s effectiveness is frequently undermined by  **Landing Page Alignment** ; if the destination URL utilizes aggressive "you" statements or dark patterns that the ad copy avoided, the system will trigger a rejection based on the multimodal scan of the entire funnel.

#### 7\. Strategic Implementation Checklist

*   **Pronoun & Proxy Audit:**  All "you/your" references and symptom-based proxies (e.g., "Can't sleep?") removed from copy.  
*   **Identity Re-Framing:**  The copy addresses "people who want" or "diverse backgrounds" rather than specific identities.  
*   **AI Disclosure Check:**  All AI-generated or modified content (visuals/audio) includes the mandatory "AI-generated" label.  
*   **Multimodal Verification:**  Text overlays, video audio, and background frames checked for inferred disability or health status.  
*   **Destination Consistency:**  Landing page claims (e.g., pricing, shipping) match the ad exactly to avoid "dark pattern" flags.  
*   **Accessibility Standards:**  Disclosures meet the 12pt/28px threshold for relevant jurisdictions.  
*   **Negative Self-Perception Check:**  Imagery avoids focused "flaw" shots or body-shaming, especially in weight loss or anti-aging.  
*   **Geographic Restriction Check:**  Crypto/DeFi campaigns exclude prohibited regions (Singapore, Belgium, etc.).
